The Brief

“Four symbols, zero clutter.”

“We need a logo that incorporates a tooth, a smile, braces and an implant.” That was the prompt EverSmile Dental brought to our design table: concise, demanding, and notoriously difficult to execute without appearing busy or fragmented.

In medical brand identity, cramming multiple anatomical or orthodontic references usually ends in graphic chaos. Rather than sidestepping the symbols with a generic swoosh, EverSmile wanted to boldly embrace them as proof of their dual specialty.

The Strategic Approach

Mathematical Synthesis over collage

Instead of stacking four separate shapes, we developed a single cohesive biomorphic vector. The outer contour forms a structured enamel tooth form; the core sweeps into a golden-ratio smile arc; micro orthodontic brackets anchor along the curve; and the lower root shafts carry medical-grade titanium implant pitch threads.

Every line serves a clinical purpose. The mark reads instantly as an authoritative crest from 10 feet away on street signage, yet reveals rich orthodontic craft on close inspection.

02 · Chromatic Architecture

Midnight Navy, Golden Sand & Clinical Precision

A calculated color system blending corporate healthcare authority with private-concierge hospitality aesthetics.

Color Compliance: WCAG 2.1 AAA Contrast Ratio (15.8:1) · Certified across Screen, Offset Lithography & Metal Signage
Color Standards: Pantone 289 C & Pantone 7509 C

Psychology of the Palette

Traditional dental branding relies heavily on sterile cyan or cold medical teal — colors patients subconsciously associate with pain and fluorescent waiting rooms.

By pairing Midnight Navy (#07142D) for deep institutional gravity with Golden Sand (#D4A574) for warm hospitality, EverSmile immediately positions itself as a premier cosmetic destination.

Material & Production Versatility

The system is tested across reverse contrast applications: etched into frosted glass, hot-stamped with gold foil on 600gsm cotton card stock, cut into 3D brushed brass channel letters, and pixel-rendered in dark mode digital interfaces.

Zero color degradation across digital and industrial physical fabrication.
